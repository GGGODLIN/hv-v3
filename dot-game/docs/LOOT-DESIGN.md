# Independent equipment system, checkpoint 0.7

This module is independently authored for dot. It does not copy or read the excluded `constants.ts`, monster dataset, pixel artwork, existing game implementation, or other authors' single-player designs. Permitted source: original-game research transcribed in [ORIGINAL-HV.md](ORIGINAL-HV.md), particularly original report 003 §1.1, §1.4, §1.5(B), and §1.6 at the linked research commit.

## Original research retained

- Quality is chosen before numeric stat rolls. Original researched R intervals overlap: Crude 0–40, Fair 30–70, Average 60–100, Superior 90–130, Exquisite 120–160, Magnificent 150–180, Legendary 170–200, Peerless 200. We choose the documented 200 upper endpoint for Legendary and disclose the source's competing 199 endpoint.
- Each generated stat rolls its own R within that quality interval. `item.rolls` stores these samples. `item.roll` is their rounded mean for a compact display, not the source of quality.
- `qualityFromRoll` is a conservative diagnostic display helper, not a reverse mapping used by generation. Because R intervals overlap, a single R cannot uniquely recover the actual quality. It picks the first compatible tier, with R=200 displayed as Peerless; callers must use `item.quality` for a generated item.
- Crude items never have a prefix or suffix. Superior and above always have a suffix. Legendary/Peerless always have both. Primary-attribute bonuses (PABs), shield-specific PAB selection, fusion, and the full original armory are **not implemented** in this checkpoint.
- Peerless+ and Ultimate are fusion progression, never ordinary generated quality tiers.
- The user-selected positive-stat interpolation is `B0 + (B200-B0)*(R/200)^1.52`, with R clamped to 0–200. Exponent 1.52 is the report's fitted model, not a claim of original server-exact code. No extrapolation above R=200 is used.
- Axe, club, and rapier attack endpoints are independently transcribed from the permitted research. Raw endpoint pairs are multiplied by fluctuation 0.0854. Ordinary/Slaughter endpoints: axe 350–701 / 470–888, club 250–621 / 353–792, rapier 150–461 / 230–601.
- Club and rapier crit endpoints are independently transcribed: ordinary 124–511, Balance club 344–991 and rapier 342–991, multiplied by 0.0105 **percentage points**, then divided by 100 for engine fractions.
- ADB scales by `1 + equipmentLevel/(50/3)`. Avoidance/crit rates scale by `1 + equipmentLevel/2000`. This version rounds flat output stats to whole units and rate fractions to four decimals; these presentation/implementation choices are not claimed to match every wiki decimal.

## Deliberate independent adaptations

The following are our new game's choices, not original HV rules and not copied from other single-player candidates:

1. Three coarse slots: weapon, armor, accessory. Accessories and the fictional base names are original content. This is not a claim to reproduce original HV's full slot model.
2. Armor and accessory endpoint tables, flat `defense`, HP bonuses, and weighting of those stats are independent first-playable balance. Flat defense level scale is 20 and bonus HP scale is 12; the combat engine documents how those translate into its original-inspired mitigation model.
3. Four new prefixes (`銳意`, `堅守`, `獵隙`, `流風`) provide small trade-off bonuses. They are not original HV prefixes. Below Legendary, eligible items independently have a 30% prefix chance; Crude remains excluded. New affix improvement is itself scaled by the item's stat-specific R curve.
4. Weapon suffixes are restricted to Slaughter, or Balance only on the independently transcribed club/rapier bases. Balance only supplies the researched crit-endpoint part in this checkpoint; accuracy is not yet an item stat. Armor permits only our new `長夜`, `壁壘`, `追風`; accessories permit those plus `破陣`. These new labels and bonuses are explicitly not an original-HV suffix catalog. Fair/Average have a 45% suffix chance; Superior+ guarantee one.
5. **Conditional on an item already being awarded**, quality weights are Crude 2%, Fair 5%, Average 35%, Superior 32%, Exquisite 18%, Magnificent 6%, Legendary 1.9%, Peerless 0.1%. Original quality-drop probabilities are unknown. This table is not a per-kill equipment-drop rate. The engine owns its separately disclosed expedition reward cadence.
6. Every item receives a tiny flat-stat comparison score; combat consumes individual stats, never that score. The score is not “true DPS” and **does not measure weapon-proc utility**, including Penetrated Armor and Stunned. It cannot prove one item is universally better, especially across weapon families. Crit, survival, and raw damage create different choices. Neither rapier nor club identity adds a hidden score bonus.
7. Individual rate bonuses are capped at 0.16 to keep generated inputs bounded at extreme imported level values. Level is sanitized to integer 1–1000, but this bound does not imply 1000 levels of complete game content.

## API and safe integration

`generateItem(rng, level, slot?)` is pure for a deterministic RNG. The module makes a bounded number of draws, clamps invalid/throwing RNG results safely, and has no wall-clock/random-global dependency. Output IDs are deterministic but are not collision-proof; the engine **must replace them with its monotonic inventory IDs**. Probability stats are fractions, e.g. 0.025 means 2.5%.

Exports: `generateItem`, `createCommissionWeapon`, `COMMISSION_WEAPON_FAMILIES`, `describeItem`, `describeItemFlavor`, `scoreItem`, `qualityFromRoll`, `qualityFactor`, `QUALITY_TIERS`, `RARITIES`, `SLOTS`, `WEAPON_FAMILIES`, `resolveWeaponFamily`, `describeWeaponTrait`, `classifySafeLootItem`, `hasUnknownSaleMetadata`, `dominanceWitness`, and `itemSalePrice`. Item extras `rolls`, `prefix`, `suffix` are optional diagnostic metadata; the existing stat interface stays compatible.

`describeItem` returns plain text. Consumers must render it as text, not as HTML from imported saves. The save validator must validate the complete item schema and never trust imported `power` as an authority.

## Weapon identity and live family descriptions

- Every newly generated weapon carries `weaponFamily: 'axe' | 'rapier' | 'club'`. Armor and accessories omit it. Family is explicit identity, not an extra stat, rarity upgrade, or new random roll. Item quality selection, affix selection, per-stat rolls, generated IDs, level scaling, weights, and flat scores remain unchanged.
- `resolveWeaponFamily(item)` is a pure, non-mutating helper. It returns a supported family only for `slot: 'weapon'`. A supported **own** `weaponFamily` is authoritative even if the display name changes. Any explicit unsupported value, including `null`, disables the family and prevents name-based fallback. Inherited metadata is not trusted.
- Only when metadata is entirely absent can a legacy weapon migrate from its **exact complete generated name**. The allowlist consists solely of this game's known quality labels, existing fictional base names, known prefixes, and legal family/quality-specific suffix combinations from v0.1/v0.2. Substring matching, translated names, whitespace normalization, arbitrary “rapier” labels, and unknown names do not grant a proc. Starter equipment has no recognized weapon family.
- The save sanitizer resolves legacy identity before changing the display name and stores the result. For unsupported identity it must preserve `weaponFamily: null` on weapons; merely dropping an invalid explicit field could accidentally enable name fallback on the next load. This sentinel survives JSON roundtrips. Non-weapons never gain a weapon proc from imported metadata.
- `describeWeaponTrait(item)` supplies current plain-text mechanism wording, including for migrated old items whose saved description predates this version. Unknown weapons explicitly say that no weapon proc is enabled. Numeric combat tuning remains in the engine's `PENETRATED_ARMOR_RULES` and `CLUB_STUN_RULES`, not duplicated as executable loot constants.
- v0.4 newly generated descriptions contain only the existing base flavor and affix notes; the UI presents the live family trait once in its separate weapon section. `describeItemFlavor(item)` removes at most one exact known appended trait suffix from a weapon's **display text**. This includes the old “club not implemented” suffix and the v0.3 rapier wording. It preserves all preceding prose, leaves non-weapons and unrecognized descriptions unchanged, and never changes the saved description or identity. This avoids duplicate or stale rules without rewriting old item descriptions. Consumers must render both helpers as text, not imported HTML.

### Penetrated Armor scope and provenance

v0.3 introduced **rapier Penetrated Armor**; v0.4 retains that selected model and adds club Stunned below. Axe retains its existing attack differences but has no implemented weapon proc. This is an incremental implementation boundary, not a claim that original HV lacked other weapon effects.

The selected historical research model has up to three Penetrated Armor stacks, each reducing the target's **original general physical mitigation** by 25%: an initial 80% mitigation becomes 60%, 40%, then 20%. This is not a flat +25% damage bonus and does not erase every defensive layer. The historical and newer English wiki descriptions disagree about duration and the triggering hit; see [ORIGINAL-HV.md](ORIGINAL-HV.md) for evidence and uncertainty. We do not describe this as a server-exact current-HV implementation.

The independent v0.3 adaptation uses a fixed 25% chance for an ordinary rapier attack that deals positive damage to a surviving target; an eligible critical hit guarantees a proc. A proc adds one stack up to three and refreshes the shared seven-tick duration, including at the cap. A new stack applies **after** the triggering damage; subsequent physical attacks and physical skills benefit, but skills do not trigger it. Fixed chance/duration, the shared refresh rule, and action eligibility are our disclosed tuning choices. The engine owns application, expiration, damage calculation, and combat save validation; see [COMBAT-DESIGN.md](COMBAT-DESIGN.md).

### v0.4 club Stunned scope and provenance

The permitted research 002 §5.2 and the Equipment Procs pages support a critical built-in proc guarantee, inability to attack/evade/parry while stunned, and no refresh of an active stun, even by a critical or another source. They do **not** say that stun disables block. The historical source describes maximum 25% chance / four ticks with weapon-level scaling; newer English wording instead bases chance on weapon type and average Base Stat Roll and omits that old duration table. Action Speed describes a free recovery action on the next player turn regardless of player speed, while its interaction with regular enemy actions is explicitly unknown. These source boundaries are recorded in [ORIGINAL-HV.md](ORIGINAL-HV.md); they are not a complete original-server specification.

The independent v0.4 adaptation uses a fixed **25% chance and four simulation ticks** for all recognized clubs. Only a basic physical hit that causes positive damage and leaves its target alive can trigger stun; positive-damage partial avoidance remains eligible. An eligible critical guarantees one stun, not one per multi-crit count. No new proc roll is consumed for an eligible critical or while the target is already stunned. Stun applies after the triggering hit; active stun cannot stack or refresh. Existing stun suppresses the target's evade/parry and ordinary attacks, but leaves block and mitigation intact. Skills, including the original-to-this-game `破勢斬`, do not trigger it.

On expiry, this game's scheduler queues one recovery action for that enemy before the next player action. While pending, that enemy does not take an ordinary attack. Recovery is tagged with the upcoming player's turn index; that enemy's ordinary attacks are suppressed while this index remains current, until the following player action advances the index. This coalescing is per enemy and based on the player-turn interval, not the caller's update chunk or substep. Skipped ordinary attacks are not banked, and neither other enemies nor the shared enemy clock are delayed or reset. Re-stunning after the recovery action is allowed, so the description must not promise permanent control or that a club makes a target unable to attack forever. Fixed rate/duration, exact eligibility, and recovery scheduling are disclosed adaptations. Full original Shield Bash, the source-described magical-resist reduction, and axe bleeding are **not implemented**. The engine owns all application, expiry, recovery, lifecycle, and combat-save safety; see [COMBAT-DESIGN.md](COMBAT-DESIGN.md).

### Comparing loot without a false universal upgrade

The UI compares flat stats separately from proc utility:

- **Axe:** the existing higher attack endpoints emphasize direct damage; no bleeding effect is promised.
- **Rapier:** lower attack endpoints can trade direct damage for later armor-reduction utility in a longer fight. PA has no direct damage benefit against zero general physical mitigation.
- **Club:** middle attack endpoints plus temporary control can suppress attack opportunities and avoidance, but block remains and expiry grants a recovery action. This is not uninterrupted safety.

The flat score does not model encounter length, current status, recovery timing, hit/critical outcomes, or enemy defenses. A lower-score weapon may therefore be useful without being universally better. No item stat endpoints, level scaling, quality/affix/drop weights, score weights, RNG draw sequence, or family migration rules changed for the v0.4 club slice.

## v0.6 conservative safe-loot comparison

This is an **independent convenience rule**, not an original-HV auto-sale rule or a claim of a universally optimal build. The six implemented positive item stats are `attack`, `defense`, `hp`, `crit`, `evade`, and `block`. No proc value, scalar score, quality rank, average roll, or item-level ordering proves dominance.

`dominanceWitness(candidate, keepers)` is a pure helper. It returns either `null` (keep the item) or detached evidence `{ keeperId, strictlyBetterKeys }`; it never mutates items, sells anything, changes gold, consumes RNG, or changes drop generation.

A witness requires **all** of these conditions:

1. The candidate is explicitly `locked: false`, has quality Crude/Fair/Average, and has a valid recognized current item shape. Superior and all higher qualities are retained, regardless of stats.
2. The caller supplies only protected keepers: currently equipped items or explicitly locked inventory items. Ownership is an engine concern; this pure helper cannot establish ownership from an item alone. A candidate whose ID matches **any** supplied keeper is rejected before comparison, even if another keeper could dominate it. The engine must also exclude equipped candidates.
3. Candidate and keeper have the **same exact recognized base** and slot. A precomputed allowlist uses this game's complete generated-name grammar: exact quality label, known prefix, base name, and legal quality/base-specific suffix. No substring extraction, translated-name inference, whitespace normalization, or new persistent base field is used. Armor's three bases remain distinct, as do the three accessory bases. Weapons additionally require the recognized name and `resolveWeaponFamily` result to agree. The combat helper's existing authoritative explicit-family behavior is unchanged; safe sale is deliberately stricter.
4. Every implemented candidate stat is less than or equal to the keeper's corresponding stat, and at least one is strictly lower. An omitted stat means zero; an explicit malformed value never means zero. Identical stats and every attack/crit/survival tradeoff are retained. Different affixes are comparable only insofar as their complete implemented stat vectors satisfy this rule on the same base.
5. Both records are ordinary own-data records with known keys. Unknown item/stat/roll metadata, unsupported identity or quality, inconsistent name/affix metadata, invalid required fields, inherited custom records, arrays, accessors, negative/nonfinite/non-numeric stats, and out-of-schema numeric values fail closed. An unknown keeper is not proof that another item is disposable. Optional historical diagnostic fields may be absent, so ordinary known legacy items can still be compared.

`classifySafeLootItem(item)` exposes this strict record/identity validation independently of low-quality or unlocked-candidate eligibility. It returns a frozen `{ slot, base, weaponFamily, quality, prefix, suffix }` identity or `null`. `hasUnknownSaleMetadata(raw)` is a narrower pre-sanitization detector for unknown or uninspectable own item/stat/roll keys, invalid supplied stat/roll values, slot-inapplicable family metadata, and malformed supplied scalar types (including non-boolean locks, nonfinite level/power/roll, and non-string text/affixes); it does not equate a custom name or high quality with an unknown key. Absent optional legacy diagnostics remain supported; established finite scalar clamping is still an engine concern. Save handling must run the relevant checks **before** discarding unknown data: sanitizing a future field away must not silently authorize disposal of that imported item. The engine rejects imports with such unsupported item/stat/roll fields or formats before sanitization instead of silently dropping it, auto-locking the result, or making that result a keeper. The existing import failure path must preserve current progress and the original source save. Unknown names or weapon families in the supported schema remain loadable but comparison-ineligible. No current check can recover metadata that an older version already discarded. Locking/ownership/import policy and user-visible warnings remain engine/UI responsibilities.

`itemSalePrice(item)` centralizes the existing `max(1, round(level * 3 + power))` formula for valid data. It is a price only, never a dominance criterion; malformed/nonfinite price inputs return zero. The engine owns all actual sale mutations, protected-keeper selection, manual preview/confirmation/revalidation, default-OFF future-drop auto-sale, and inventory/pending-loot lifecycle. Manual and automatic disposal must reuse the same witness predicate rather than inventing a score shortcut. This module adds no save fields, archive/quarantine arrays, durable telemetry, or new equipment rolls.

## v0.7 independent fixed-spec weapon commissions

This is an **independently designed solo-game commission service**, not a reproduction of original HV's Bazaar replenishment, player-supplied shop stock, Training, or Forge. Its purpose is a predictable credit sink and access to the three implemented weapon families, not a random reroll, an equipment upgrade service, or a promise of a universally stronger build. No stock timers, rotating inventory, commissions ledger, forging progression, or original-server prices are implied.

`COMMISSION_WEAPON_FAMILIES` is a frozen array of frozen `{ family, label }` descriptors in axe / rapier / club order. `createCommissionWeapon(family, level)` returns a new ordinary item for a supported family and an **integer hero level 1–500**; all other inputs return `null` without coercion or clamping. This stricter supported hero-level boundary is separate from the historical random generator's import-safe level clamp.

Every commission has exactly these fixed properties:

- Current supplied hero level, Superior quality, **R110 for every applicable stat**, Slaughter suffix (`屠戮`), and no prefix. Axe has attack; rapier and club have attack plus their ordinary crit endpoint range. Slaughter changes only the existing researched attack endpoints; it does not grant Balance crit endpoints.
- The same existing quality interpolation, endpoint selection, level scales, flat/rate rounding, probability cap, and `scoreItem` calculation as random loot. A private shared assembler constructs both paths. No second damage formula, extra weapon-proc bonus, or commission-specific combat stat exists.
- `locked: true` for safe delivery. There are **no new item fields**: no provenance, receipt, forging, stock, purchase-price, or commission marker is persisted. The ordinary generated name, explicit family, affix metadata, and schema remain recognizable by strict safe-loot classification. Unknown-field/import guards are unchanged.
- A stable preview-only ID `commission-${family}-${level}`. The engine must overwrite it with its standard collision-safe inventory ID at commit; two purchases must never reuse the preview ID. The helper allocates fresh item, stats, and rolls objects on every call and never reads game state, RNG, or the clock. The same family/level always has the same complete preview and stats, and mutating one result does not affect later results.

The engine owns purchase eligibility, live revalidation, atomic payment/delivery, inventory capacity, standard ID allocation, and explicit user confirmation. The commission's disclosed independent price is `max(250, 4 * itemSalePrice(canonicalItem))`; the existing ordinary resale formula is unchanged. Price is not a persisted stat or a claim about original HV economics. The helper does not spend gold, equip the weapon, award random drops, or change safe-sale settings. Even after unlocking, a Superior commission remains outside the low-quality conservative safe-sale rule.

At level 7 the exact fixed outputs are:

| Family | Attack | Item crit bonus | Flat score | Ordinary resale | Commission price |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Axe | 77 | — | 154 | 175 | 700 |
| Rapier | 46 | 2.95% | 100 | 121 | 484 |
| Club | 64 | 2.95% | 136 | 157 | 628 |

These are comparison numbers, not equivalent combat utility: rapier PA and club Stunned still use their existing engine rules, while axe still has no implemented proc. A fixed commission can be worse than already owned loot; no reroll or guaranteed-upgrade claim is appropriate.
